How we help

Every journey begins with understanding the client's unique needs. We start with a comprehensive assessment, followed by personalised therapy. We support individuals at every stage of life.

Speech

Helping children and adults speak more clearly and confidently

  • Speech sound delays and disorders
  • Motor speech disorders, including apraxia and dysarthria
  • Stuttering and fluency disorders
  • Acquired speech difficulties following stroke or brain injury

Language

Supporting understanding and use of language for everyday life and learning

  • Developmental language delays and disorders in children
  • Acquired language disorders (aphasia) following stroke or brain injury
  • Social communication difficulties
  • Language and communication changes associated with neurological conditions

Literacy

Building the skills needed to read, write and succeed at school

  • Early literacy skills, including phonological awareness and phonics
  • Reading accuracy, fluency and comprehension
  • Spelling
  • Written expression

Voice

Supporting clearer, stronger and more comfortable voices

  • Voice disorders
  • Vocal strain or fatigue
  • Voice rehabilitation following laryngectomy
  • Voice changes associated with neurological conditions

Swallowing

Supporting safe and effective swallowing

  • Swallowing difficulties following stroke or brain injury
  • Swallowing difficulties associated with neurological conditions
  • Swallowing difficulties following surgery or head and neck cancer treatments
  • Paediatric feeding and swallowing difficulties

Conditions

Who we often help

You don't need a diagnosis to get in touch. If something doesn't feel right, an assessment is a good place to start.

Late talking

Toddlers who are using fewer words, gestures or word combinations than expected for their age.

Speech delays and disorders

Children whose speech is difficult to understand or who struggle to produce certain sounds.

Language delays and disorders

Children who have difficulty understanding language, following instructions or expressing their thoughts and ideas.

Dysfluency

Children and adults who experience stuttering or disruptions in the flow of speech.

Learning difficulties and dyslexia

Children experiencing difficulties with language-based learning, following instructions, and/or literacy.

Autism

Autistic children who would benefit from support with communication and social interaction.

Down syndrome

Children with Down syndrome who experience speech, language or communication difficulties.

Stroke and brain injury

Individuals experiencing changes in communication, speech or swallowing following a stroke or brain injury.

Head and neck cancer

Individuals experiencing speech, voice or swallowing difficulties following oral, throat or laryngeal cancer or its treatment.

Progressive neurological conditions

Adults living with conditions such as Parkinson's disease or dementia, which can affect communication, voice and swallowing over time.

The conditions listed above are just some examples of the individuals and difficulties we work with. Every person's needs are unique, and we welcome enquiries about other speech, language, communication, voice and swallowing concerns. If you're unsure whether speech therapy could help, we're happy to chat!

Home › For parents

Is your child's speech on track?

If you're worried about your child's speech and language development, you may have heard the advice to "wait and see", but waiting isn't always the best approach. Early identification of speech and language difficulties allows children to access the support they need when it matters most. If you have concerns, don't hesitate to seek advice.

By 4 years oldchildren should be understood by both familiar and unfamiliar listeners most of the time.

If people outside your family often struggle to understand your child's speech, it may be worth having their speech assessed.

Signs to look out for, by age

At 1 year old

  • Not yet saying first words
  • Not yet using gestures like pointing or waving
  • Only makes a few sounds

At 2 years old

  • Not yet following one step instructions
  • Not yet putting two words together
  • Not yet naming familiar objects when asked

At 3 years old

  • Not yet following two step instructions
  • Not yet asking questions
  • Not yet using simple three word sentences

At 4 to 5 years old

  • Not yet able to follow instructions at school
  • Not yet speaking in sentences
  • Not yet understood by strangers
  • Leaves out sounds or uses immature sounds, like "tat" for "cat"
  • Gets frustrated when trying to communicate

School aged children

Some children develop basic communication skills but struggle with the academic language they need once they reach school. Look out for difficulty with:

  • Following instructions in class
  • Listening or reading comprehension
  • Reading or spelling
  • Spoken or written language

Helpful reads for parents

Short guides based on the questions families ask us most.

Why "wait and see" isn't good advice

Some children catch up on their own, but many don't.

Read more

It's common to hear "they'll grow out of it". Some children do catch up naturally, but many do not, and it's hard to tell which group your child is in.

Early intervention can make a big difference to communication development, and it reduces frustration for both children and parents. An assessment either gives you reassurance or shows exactly what support is needed.

Speech therapy for school aged children

When everyday communication seems fine, but learning becomes a challenge.

Read more

Sometimes children develop basic communication skills, but once they reach school they struggle with the academic language they need to succeed.

These difficulties may affect a child's ability to follow classroom instructions, understand and answer questions, develop reading comprehension, spell, write and express their ideas clearly.

We provide comprehensive assessments and individualised therapy to support the language, literacy and communication skills children need to participate, learn and thrive at school.

How clear should my child's speech be?

A simple rule of thumb for 4 year olds.

Read more

By 4 years old, children should generally be understood by familiar and unfamiliar listeners most of the time, even though some speech sounds may still be developing.

Signs to look out for: people frequently struggle to understand your child, your child leaves out or changes sounds in words, uses immature speech patterns (for example saying "tat" instead of "cat"), becomes frustrated when trying to communicate, or their speech clarity doesn't seem to be improving over time.

Will learning two languages confuse my child?

Understanding bilingual language development and why your home language matters.

Read more

Growing up with more than one language is a wonderful gift. Many parents worry that learning two (or more) languages may confuse their child or delay speech and language development. However, learning more than one language does not cause speech or language delays.

Your home language is the foundation for communication, connection, culture and family relationships. Strong language skills in a child's first language support the development of additional languages.

You should never give up your home language to help your child learn another. Give your child the gift of both!

How it works, fees and FAQs

Everything you need to know before your first appointment.

How it works

  1. Get in touch

    Send an enquiry or WhatsApp us on 076 561 1233. Tell us a little about your concerns. You don't need a referral.

  2. A quick chat

    We'll get back to you to talk through your concerns and book an assessment.

  3. Assessment

    A comprehensive assessment of speech, language, literacy, voice, or swallowing, depending on your concerns and the individual's needs. Assessment duration varies depending on the type of assessment.

  4. Feedback and recommendations

    We'll discuss the assessment findings, explain any areas of difficulty and recommend the next steps. Together, we'll establish goals and a plan moving forward.

  5. Therapy

    Individualised therapy tailored to the client's goals and needs, with practical strategies and activities to encourage progress beyond the therapy room.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a referral?
No. A doctor's referral is not required. You can contact us directly to arrange an assessment.
What ages do you work with?
We provide speech-language therapy for all ages, from babies and toddlers to school-aged children, teenagers and adults.
How long is a session?
We offer 30, 45 and 60-minute sessions, depending on the individual's age, needs and therapy goals. The most appropriate session length will be discussed with you.
Do you accept medical aid?
We are a private, cash practice and do not submit claims directly to medical aids. Payment is made directly to the practice, and paid invoices can be submitted to your medical aid for possible reimbursement, depending on your plan and available benefits.
How many sessions will be needed?
This varies from person to person. Some individuals may benefit from a short period of therapy, while others may require ongoing support. Following an assessment, we'll discuss our recommendations and work together to establish a suitable therapy plan. Progress is reviewed regularly.
Which languages do you provide therapy in?
Our practice currently provides therapy in English only. For children who speak another home language, we encourage families to seek support from a bilingual speech-language therapist where possible. Supporting a child's home language is important for their overall language development, and families are encouraged to continue using it at home.
My child is very young. Is it too early for speech therapy?
Not at all! Early support can make a meaningful difference to a child's communication development. If you have concerns about your child's speech, language or communication, you don't need to wait until they are older. An assessment can help determine whether support is needed.
Do you provide written reports?
Yes! Written assessment reports are available upon request and are charged for separately. Please let us know if you require a report when booking your assessment.
What is your cancellation policy?
We understand that plans can change! One cancellation per month is permitted without charge, provided notice is received before 8:00 am on the day of the appointment. Additional cancellations within the same month will incur a fee of 50% of the session fee if notice is received before 8:00 am, or 100% if notice is received after 8:00 am. We ask that clients let us know as soon as possible if unable to attend their appointment.
Where are you based?
Our practice is located at The Hope Centre, 31 Raleigh Road, Amanzimtoti. We also offer therapy at selected local schools, and online via video call.

Privacy notice

How we collect, use, store and protect your personal information in accordance with the Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA).

Who we are

Stacey Wall Speech-Language Therapy (Practice Number: 1142399), based at The Hope Centre, 31 Raleigh Road, Amanzimtoti, 4126, is responsible for the personal information you share with us.

We are committed to protecting your privacy and treating all personal and clinical information with confidentiality and care.

What information we collect

Depending on the services you receive, we may collect:

  • Personal details, including names, dates of birth and contact information.
  • Relevant medical, developmental and educational histories.
  • Assessment results, clinical observations and therapy notes.
  • Reports, referrals and information provided by other healthcare professionals.
  • Billing and payment information.
  • Communications between you and our practice.

When you contact us through our website enquiry form, we collect the information you provide, such as your name, contact details and a brief description of your enquiry.

We ask that you avoid including detailed medical or sensitive clinical information in website enquiry forms.

Why we collect your information

We collect and process personal information for the purposes of:

  • Responding to enquiries and arranging appointments.
  • Conducting assessments and providing appropriate therapy.
  • Maintaining accurate clinical records and monitoring progress.
  • Communicating with patients, parents and caregivers.
  • Preparing reports and correspondence when required.
  • Managing appointments, invoicing and practice administration.
  • Meeting our professional, ethical and legal obligations.

We only collect information that is relevant and necessary for these purposes.

How we store and protect your information

We take reasonable steps to protect your personal and clinical information against loss, unauthorised access, disclosure or misuse.

Patient records are stored in:

  • Physical files: Kept in a locked filing cupboard with restricted access.
  • Electronic records: Stored securely through Bookem, our password-protected, cloud-based practice management system.

We take care to ensure that access to confidential information is limited to authorised individuals involved in providing or managing your care.

Communication and confidentiality

  • We communicate with patients and families through WhatsApp, email, SMS and the Bookem practice management system.
  • We take care to protect confidentiality when communicating electronically. However, electronic communication carries some privacy risks, which are outside our complete control.
  • We will not disclose your personal or clinical information to third parties without appropriate consent, unless disclosure is required or permitted by law, or necessary for your care.
  • Where relevant, information may be shared with other healthcare professionals involved in your care, with your permission.
  • Our practice does not routinely submit claims directly to medical aids. If you request documentation for reimbursement purposes, relevant information may be included in the documents provided to you.

Third-party service providers

We use trusted service providers, including Bookem and electronic communication providers, to assist with practice administration and communication.

These providers may process or store personal information on our behalf. We take reasonable steps to ensure that appropriate confidentiality and security safeguards are in place.

How long we keep your information

Clinical records are retained in accordance with HPCSA guidelines and applicable legislation. Patient records are ideally retained indefinitely. Where this is not practical, records are kept for at least six years after they become dormant (when the patient was last treated).

Longer retention periods apply to certain patient groups, including:

  • Children: Records are retained at least until the patient's 21st birthday.
  • Patients who are mentally incapacitated: Records are retained for the duration of their lifetime.

Enquiry information relating to individuals who do not become clients is retained only for as long as reasonably necessary for the purpose for which it was collected, subject to applicable legal requirements.

Your rights

In terms of POPIA, you have the right to:

  • Request confirmation of whether we hold personal information about you.
  • Request access to your personal and clinical records.
  • Request the correction of inaccurate or incomplete information.
  • Request deletion of personal information where legally permissible.
  • Object to certain types of processing.
  • Withdraw consent where processing is based on consent.

Please note that certain clinical records must be retained to meet our professional and legal obligations, even if deletion is requested.

To exercise these rights, please contact us using the details below.

Children and parental consent

When providing services to children, we collect and process personal information necessary for their assessment and therapy.

Where required, consent is obtained from a parent, legal guardian or other legally authorised person. We take particular care to protect children's personal and clinical information.

Security breaches

Should we become aware of a security compromise involving your personal information, we will take appropriate steps to address the incident and notify the Information Regulator and affected individuals as required by POPIA.
